Project Introduction

What is Faceo?

Multi-Task Facial Intelligence Platform

Faceo is an integrated, modular facial analysis and empirical evaluation framework specifically engineered to address the critical vulnerabilities of modern computer vision systems when deployed on Sri Lankan and South Asian demographic populations.

While widely adopted client-side facial AI libraries like default Face-API.js achieve strong results on Western benchmarks, they suffer from severe demographic bias (Fitzpatrick IV & V skin tones), catastrophic accuracy drops under real-world low illumination (< 15 Lux), and susceptibility to hyper-realistic synthetic deepfakes. Faceo bridges these foundational gaps by combining local preprocessing pipelines, lightweight client-side edge architectures, and rigorous multi-model forensic validation into a single, cohesive research platform.

Privacy-Preserving Edge Inference

Executes real-time age, gender, and feature detection directly inside client browsers via Face-API.js (WebGL/WASM) without uploading raw biometric video feeds to cloud servers.

Adaptive Illumination Pipeline

Recovers high-frequency textural boundaries and micro-expressions in sub-15 Lux environments using dynamic CLAHE and parametric Gamma correction before classification.

Phenotype & Culture Tailored

Tackles demographic phenotype bias across South Asian skin tones (Fitzpatrick IV & V) and regional facial morphology often neglected in Western training corpora.

Forensic Generalization Defense

Combines Error Level Analysis with modern vision architectures (ResNet-50 vs. Swin vs. ViT) to prevent benchmark overfitting and reliably flag unseen out-of-distribution deepfakes.

Empirical Research Gap

Core Problem Statement

01

Demographic Generalization Deficit

Automated facial analysis systems struggle with demographic generalization when deployed on underrepresented populations such as Sri Lankan and South Asian faces.

02

Low-Illumination Feature Degradation

Reduced illumination degrades facial feature extraction, impacting real-time emotion recognition.

03

Benchmark Complacency & Distribution Shift

Near-perfect within-dataset benchmark performance often conceals severe generalization drops on unseen out-of-distribution imagery (e.g., in deepfake detection).

Research Motivation & Objectives

Addressing Critical Blind Spots in Global Facial AI

When standard computer vision systems trained predominantly on Western benchmarks are applied to Sri Lankan populations, severe degradation arises across demographic, illumination, and forensic axes.

Challenge 01

Demographic Phenotype Bias

Western-centric training datasets lead to significant degradation on South Asian phenotypes (Melanin IV & V).

Most mainstream facial analysis systems are trained on web-scraped Caucasian datasets (CelebA, LFW). When deployed on South Asian and Sri Lankan demographics with higher melanin concentrations and distinct facial morphology, landmark estimation shifts and facial expression feature extractors misclassify subtle emotional signals.

14-22%
Accuracy disparity on non-adapted models
Challenge 02

Unconstrained Low-Light Degradation

Real-world illumination drops below 15 Lux, triggering catastrophic loss of discriminative spatial high frequencies.

In unconstrained environments across Sri Lanka—such as municipal cameras, poorly lit indoor settings, and nighttime surveillance—low illumination and severe photon noise mask micro-expressions. Standard deep learning backbones fail to extract edge contours and textural cues without specialized dynamic enhancement.

38.7%
Performance drop under < 15 Lux without CLAHE
Challenge 03

The Out-of-Distribution Generalization Gap

High-capacity Vision Transformers scoring ~99% on source benchmarks collapse on unseen real-world deepfakes.

While state-of-the-art transformer backbones boast near-perfect performance on standard benchmark test partitions (e.g. FaceForensics++), they severely overfit to benchmark-specific compression artifacts and forensic noise signatures, failing when applied to novel cross-dataset deepfake distributions.

-18.88%
Swin Transformer drop on 2,000 unseen images
Core Methodology

Primary Research Objectives

To overcome aggregate score masking—where high performance in one domain obscures vulnerabilities in another—this project establishes strict component-level benchmarking.

Decoupled Component-Wise Evaluation

Avoiding aggregate score masking by independently evaluating each analytical task—FER, demographics, skin conditions, and deepfakes—against isolated stressors.

Localized Illumination & Phenotype Adaptation

Integrating Contrast Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization (CLAHE) and parametric Gamma correction to recover critical facial gradients on South Asian skin tones.

Privacy-Preserving Edge Feasibility

Demonstrating sub-40ms demographic estimation directly inside the client browser using WebAssembly and Face-API.js, eliminating biometric cloud transmission risks.

Empirical Cross-Dataset Generalization

Benchmarking convolutional inductive biases (ResNet-50) against Vision Transformers (ViT, Swin) with Error Level Analysis to establish reliable forensic generalization.
